Understanding EUIPO official fees.

What the application fee relates to, why classes matter and how professional fees fit alongside it.

The processSources checked 17 October 2026

General educational information. This article does not assess your trademark or replace advice on your circumstances.

Keep professional fees and official fees separate when comparing services. The official application fee is not the total price of professional assistance.

Two different kinds of cost.

The official application fee relates to the EUIPO procedure. A professional service fee pays for the work agreed with a service provider. They should be explained separately.

The service scope matters when comparing an offer. A filing instruction may not include comprehensive clearance, responses to objections, opposition or ongoing monitoring.

Why classes enter the calculation.

Goods and services are grouped into classes. EUIPO’s online application fee for an individual trademark includes one class, with further charges for a second and each additional class.

The Pricing page includes a calculator using the published official schedule. It is not a classification tool and does not tell you which classes your business needs. Always check the current EUIPO schedule before filing.

A fee is not an outcome.

Paying an application fee does not guarantee registration. The application still follows the EUIPO procedure, and issues such as objections or opposition may arise.

Any additional professional work should be discussed within an expressly agreed scope, rather than assumed to be part of the initial filing service.

Ask what the figure includes.

Before instructing a service, look for a clear explanation of the professional fee, the official fee, applicable tax treatment and any separate costs.

MARKEE’s professional fees are separate from official fees. Confirm the scope, applicable taxes and any additional work before instruction.

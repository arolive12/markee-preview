Before you apply: three questions worth asking.

The sign, the scope and the earlier rights. A little preparation gives the next conversation more focus.

Getting startedSources checked 17 October 2026

Prepare the questions before looking for a conclusion. A quick check and a comprehensive clearance search are not the same service.

What exactly is the mark?

Write down the brand name or identify the sign you want to use. If there is a logo as well as a word mark, consider which version you are asking about.

Distinctiveness is an important part of trademark examination. The question depends on the sign and the goods or services concerned; a familiar-looking result for another mark does not settle your application.

What will it cover?

Describe what your business actually sells or provides. Goods and services are grouped into classes, but the wording within the application matters as well as the number of classes.

A considered list should reflect your business and realistic plans. EUIPO recommends clear and precise terms, rather than an unnecessarily long list.

What might already be there?

Searches can help identify earlier trademark applications or registrations that deserve attention. Finding a similar mark does not, on its own, answer whether there is a conflict.

Relevant signs, activities, territories and other circumstances need consideration. Comprehensive clearance is a separately scoped search and analysis exercise, and it cannot eliminate every possible risk.

Bring context to the next step.

For an initial conversation, a short description of the mark, the business activities and the intended territory is a useful place to start.

MARKEE’s free preliminary assessment is limited. Comprehensive clearance and registration each need their own agreed scope. Neither guarantees availability or registration.

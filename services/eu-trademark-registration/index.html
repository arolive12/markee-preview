EU-wide protection. A defined scope.

An EU trademark registration can provide protection across EU member states for the mark and the goods or services it covers. It does not give exclusive rights to a brand name for every type of business.

The mark
The sign you apply for matters. A word mark and a logo can raise different questions about representation and protection.
The goods and services
Your application defines what the mark will cover. The wording needs to reflect your business and the protection you seek.
The territory
An EU trademark covers EU member states. Protection elsewhere requires separate consideration.

The thinking before the filing.

Getting these decisions right helps give your application a clearer foundation.

  1. Establish ownership.

    Confirm the person or legal entity that should own the trademark, and check the applicant details before filing.

  2. Consider the mark.

    Think about distinctiveness and possible earlier rights. A company name or domain registration does not itself establish trademark availability.

  3. Define your activities.

    Describe the products and services you provide and your realistic plans. Comprehensive clearance, if instructed, should be aligned with the proposed scope.

From preliminary assessment to EU application.

A request to MARKEE is not itself an EUIPO application. Registration involves agreeing the scope, confirming the details and approving the filing.

  1. 01

    Review the preliminary assessment

    Consider the professionally validated preliminary results and decide whether to proceed. A more detailed search may be appropriate; no paid assessment is automatically required.

  2. 02

    Confirm the scope and fees

    Agree the registration service scope and professional fees, separately from official EUIPO fees.

  3. 03

    Confirm the application details

    Provide or confirm additional applicant, trademark and goods/services information. Relevant information from the check can inform the application, subject to the agreed data-handling arrangements.

  4. 04

    Complete the engagement requirements

    Complete the required engagement, authorisation and identity verification steps.

  5. 05

    Prepare the application

    MARKEE prepares the application within the agreed professional service scope.

  6. 06

    Review and approve the filing

    The customer reviews and approves the proposed application before filing.

  7. 07

    Pay applicable official fees and file

    Applicable official fees are paid and the application is submitted to EUIPO through the approved filing process.

  8. 08

    Receive filing confirmation

    Filing confirmation and relevant next-stage information are provided. EUIPO examines the application; objections or opposition may require separately agreed work. Registration is not guaranteed.

Before you decide.

Is clearance included in registration?

+

Not automatically. The free preliminary assessment is limited in scope, and comprehensive clearance is a separate search and analysis service. Any clearance work must be expressly included in the agreed instruction.

Does an EU trademark protect me outside the EU?

+

An EU trademark covers EU member states. If you need protection in other territories, that requires separate consideration and may involve other applications.

What happens if an objection or opposition is raised?

+

The issue needs to be reviewed before deciding how to respond. This work is not automatically included in the filing service. Its scope and fees would be agreed separately.

Does a request to MARKEE file an application?

+

No. A request to MARKEE is separate from an application to the trademark office. The filing follows agreement of the professional scope, confirmation of the details and approval of the application.
